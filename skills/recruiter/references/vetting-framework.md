# Candidate Vetting Framework

Use with `decision-framework.md`. This file defines risk checks and interview probes, not client-specific preferences.

## Hard gates

- Mandatory licence, certification, clearance, education, or work status is supported.
- Recent experience covers the core work named in the JD.
- Location, shift, travel, and work model are acceptable to the candidate.
- Compensation range and candidate target do not conflict.
- No verified ownership, do-not-contact, duplicate-submission, or conflict restriction blocks the action.

Any unsupported hard gate produces **NEEDS VERIFICATION**, not a guessed pass.

## Scope checks

- What did the candidate personally own?
- What decisions could they make without escalation?
- What people, vendors, budgets, systems, lines, shifts, or sites were in scope?
- Which tools, equipment, standards, or software did they use directly?
- What broke, changed, improved, or failed, and what did they do?
- Which numbers are explicitly supported by resume or call evidence?

## Job-change pattern

Read chronology with context:

- Long stable tenure followed by short local contracts is not automatically job hopping.
- Repeated sub-year permanent roles need direct explanation.
- Progressive titles and expanded scope are stronger than title repetition.
- Layoff, plant closure, relocation, contract end, and promotion must remain distinct.

Do not infer motivation. Ask and record the candidate's stated reason.

## Risk flags

- Title suggests leadership but evidence shows coordination only.
- Claims rely on team outcomes with no personal ownership.
- Core system/equipment exposure is old or indirect.
- Required credential is expired, unclear, or absent.
- Compensation, commute, shift, or notice conflicts with the role.
- Resume and call timeline disagree.
- Candidate is over-levelled or under-levelled for actual scope.
- Client-specific requirement appears only in memory, not current approved sources.

## Focused probes

Ask only questions that can change the decision:

1. "What did you personally own in that process?"
2. "Which equipment or systems did you use directly, and how recently?"
3. "How many people, shifts, lines, or sites were in your scope?"
4. "Tell me about a failure or urgent problem you had authority to solve."
5. "Which credential supports this requirement, and is it current?"
6. "What location, shift, compensation, and start conditions are firm?"

## Fact integrity

- Resume is baseline career record.
- Transcript can update it only when the candidate explicitly said so.
- JD controls positioning, not fact creation.
- Call notes are supporting context and must align with transcript when both exist.
- Missing or conflicting facts are flagged for human review.
