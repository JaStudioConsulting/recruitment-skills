# Reference check writing standard

## Evidence hierarchy

1. The reference-call transcript or direct reference notes control what the referee said.
2. The candidate resume or verified candidate record controls baseline employment, progression, education, and title facts.
3. The job record controls the applied role, client, and relevant emphasis.
4. Recruiter notes may supplement facts when they are clear and consistent.

Do not use the resume to manufacture the referee's opinion. Do not create a rating, recommendation, development area, rehire intent, or factual example that the referee did not support.

## Ja's writing pattern

Write as a recruiter translating a real call, not as an HR policy writer.

- Direct, formal, and favourable when the evidence is favourable.
- Specific before promotional.
- Human and natural, using the candidate's and referee's first names.
- Confident only to the degree supported by the call.
- Built around progression, concrete responsibilities, behaviour, and examples.
- Free of generic praise such as `exceptional`, `world-class`, or `perfect fit` unless the referee used equivalent language.

Preferred answer pattern:

`Derek described Trevor as intelligent, mechanically capable, and quick to learn. He highlighted Trevor's progression from hands-on machine work into complex progressive-die design and simulation support.`

For yes/no questions, lead clearly:

`Yes. Derek stated that he would take Trevor back immediately and that Trevor would have a job there tomorrow if he wanted one.`

For direct language with real impact, preserve the phrase:

`Derek said Trevor was a major loss to the company and left difficult shoes to fill.`

## Building the material

- Mine the full call before drafting. A referee may answer later questions while discussing an earlier topic.
- Put the strongest evidence where it naturally belongs: progression under adaptability, attendance under dependability, technical complexity under problem-solving, and direct rehire language under rehire.
- Use the professional relationship field to explain who the referee was, what part of the operation they managed, and how closely they observed the candidate's work.
- Keep the duration answer short when the relationship field already carries the detail.
- Build three to five non-overlapping strengths. Combine related technical facts instead of creating repetitive bullets.
- Treat the improvement answer honestly. If no weakness was provided and the referee repeatedly said there was nothing negative to report, say exactly that.
- If no numerical performance rating was given, say so, then summarize the supported strength of the endorsement. Never infer a number.
- Use one concrete example in full when available. Do not flatten every answer into broad adjectives.
- Consolidate repeated endorsements into the final additional-comments answer.

## Editing checks

- Spell names and companies consistently.
- Use one applied-role title unless the source clearly distinguishes another role.
- Preserve official titles and meaningful technical terms.
- Convert conversational fragments into complete sentences without changing meaning.
- Preserve uncertainty, qualifications, ranges, and mixed feedback.
- Avoid repeating the same sentence or example in more than two answers.
- Use Canadian business English naturally.
- Do not add private personal details unless they materially support a reference answer and were explicitly discussed.
